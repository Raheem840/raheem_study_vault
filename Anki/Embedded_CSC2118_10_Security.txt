#deck:Embedded::CSC2118::10 Security
Give two reasons embedded security is harder than server security.	Embedded devices are often physically accessible to attackers (can be probed/desoldered), have limited compute for heavy cryptography, and are deployed in the field for years without easy updates
What does Secure Boot verify, and how is trust established from power-on?	It verifies that firmware is cryptographically signed by the developer before running it, via a chain of trust starting from an immutable first-stage bootloader burned into the chip's silicon/eFuses
Why do you need Flash Encryption in addition to Secure Boot, not instead of it?	Secure Boot only stops unauthorized firmware from running; it doesn't stop an attacker with physical access from reading the flash chip's contents directly. Flash Encryption protects data at rest, so both together cover both attack angles
What does TLS protect against, and what ESP-IDF library implements it?	Eavesdropping and man-in-the-middle attacks on network communication, by encrypting data in transit and verifying server identity via certificates; implemented via mbedTLS in ESP-IDF
What two conditions make an OTA (over-the-air) update mechanism secure?	The update must be fetched over TLS (confidentiality/integrity in transit) AND the new firmware image must be signed and verified by Secure Boot before being accepted
What does NVS encryption protect specifically?	Key-value data stored in the Non-Volatile Storage partition, such as Wi-Fi credentials and calibration data, built on top of Flash Encryption
Name three concrete attack surfaces on a deployed IoT device and their mitigations.	Physical flash access (mitigated by Flash Encryption), malicious firmware flashing (mitigated by Secure Boot), network eavesdropping (mitigated by TLS) — also valid: insecure OTA (signed firmware + TLS) or debug/JTAG left enabled (disable in production)
What happens to the Secure Boot signing key digest once burned into eFuses on ESP32-S3?	It becomes a permanent, one-time-programmable hardware lock that cannot be undone
