#deck:Embedded::CSC2118::09 Serial Communication
Why is serial communication (one bit at a time) often preferred over parallel, despite seeming slower?	Fewer wires means simpler, cheaper hardware, and it avoids signal skew issues that limit parallel communication speed over longer distances
What makes UART 'asynchronous,' and what must both devices agree on beforehand?	There's no shared clock line; both devices must independently be configured to the same baud rate (bits per second) beforehand for correct framing
List UART's frame components in order.	Start bit, data bits, optional parity bit, stop bit(s)
What are SCL and SDA in I2C, and why does I2C need external pull-up resistors?	SCL is the shared serial clock, SDA is the shared bidirectional data line; pull-up resistors are needed because the I2C bus is open-drain — devices can only pull the line LOW, so resistors pull it back HIGH
How does I2C address multiple devices on just 2 wires?	Each device has a unique 7-bit (or 10-bit) address; the master specifies which address it wants to communicate with before sending/receiving data
How does SPI select a specific device among multiple slaves?	By physically driving that device's unique Chip Select (CS) line LOW — each slave needs its own dedicated CS wire from the master
Name SPI's four typical signal lines.	SCLK (clock), MOSI (Master Out Slave In), MISO (Master In Slave Out), CS/SS (Chip Select, one per slave)
Which of UART, I2C, and SPI is full-duplex and generally the fastest?	SPI
Which of UART, I2C, and SPI supports multiple devices sharing just 2 wires via unique addresses?	I2C
Two UART devices are wired without swapping TX/RX and have mismatched baud rates — predict the result.	Both TX-to-TX/RX-to-RX wiring and mismatched baud rates would prevent correct communication; specifically mismatched baud rate produces garbled/gibberish received characters even if wiring were correct
