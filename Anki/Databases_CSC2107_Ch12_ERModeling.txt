#deck:Databases::CSC2107::Ch12 ER Modeling
Distinguish entity type from entity occurrence.	Entity type is the category/class (e.g. Staff); entity occurrence is one specific, uniquely identifiable instance of that type (e.g. one particular staff member)
Define a unary (recursive) relationship, with an example.	A relationship that relates an entity type to ITSELF; example: Staff Supervises Staff
Define a ternary relationship.	A relationship of degree 3, relating three entity types simultaneously, which can't always be decomposed into simpler binary relationships without losing information
Distinguish a composite attribute from a multi-valued attribute.	Composite: made up of smaller sub-parts (e.g. address = street+city+postcode); multi-valued: can have multiple values per entity occurrence (e.g. multiple phone numbers)
What is a derived attribute? Give an example.	An attribute computed from other attribute(s) rather than stored directly, e.g. age derived from dateOfBirth
Distinguish a strong entity type from a weak entity type.	A strong entity type's existence doesn't depend on another entity and has its own candidate key; a weak entity type's existence depends on an owner entity and has no candidate key of its own, identified via a partial key plus the owner's key
Can a relationship type itself have attributes? Give an example.	Yes — e.g. Staff Manages Branch could have a dateStarted attribute belonging to the relationship itself, not to either entity alone
Distinguish cardinality from participation in structural constraints.	Cardinality describes the MAXIMUM number of relationship occurrences an entity can be involved in (1:1, 1:*, *:*); participation describes whether an entity MUST participate (mandatory) or not (optional) — the minimum
What does (0,1) notation mean for a relationship's participation/cardinality?	Optional participation (minimum 0) with a maximum cardinality of one
Define a fan trap and its fix.	Occurs when one entity connects to two other entities via 1:* relationships but there's no real direct relationship between those two 'many' entities, incorrectly implying connections; fixed by adding a direct relationship if one genuinely exists, or restructuring
Define a chasm trap and its fix.	Occurs when a model implies a relationship path exists between entities, but an optional participation somewhere along the path means the path isn't guaranteed for every occurrence, losing information; fixed by adding a missing direct relationship
